Title: Gridfall

High-Level Concept:
A clean, fast falling-block puzzle game for Android phones — a modern take on
the classic stacking puzzle. It is a flat 2d game held in portrait, played with
one thumb. There is no player character and no avatar: nothing walks, nothing
drives, nothing is piloted. The player never moves through a world. The whole
game is a puzzle board on the glass, and the only things that move are the
falling blocks the player steers with taps and swipes. No 3d scenery of any
kind.

The Board
One tall rectangular well, ten cells wide and twenty cells tall, drawn as a
recessed panel with a faint grid. Blocks lock into that grid and stack from the
bottom up. To the right of the well, a narrow column of small readout panels:
a Next box showing the two upcoming pieces, a Hold box for the one piece the
player has set aside, a Score readout, a Lines readout and a Level readout.
Along the top, a thin status bar. Nothing else is on screen.

The Pieces
Seven block shapes, each four cells in size and each its own flat colour: a
straight bar in cyan, a square in yellow, a T-shape in purple, an S-shape in
green, a Z-shape in red, an L-shape in orange and a J-shape in blue. Pieces
enter at the top of the well one at a time, in a shuffled bag so the same seven
always arrive before any repeats. A pale ghost outline sits at the bottom of
the well showing exactly where the current piece will land.

What the Player Does
Everything is touch. Swipe left or right to shift the falling piece one column.
Tap the left or right half of the well to nudge it the same way. Tap the piece
itself to rotate it a quarter turn clockwise; press and hold two fingers to
rotate the other way. Swipe down to soft-drop, which speeds the fall while the
finger is held. Flick down hard to hard-drop, which slams the piece straight to
the ghost outline and locks it instantly. Swipe up to send the current piece to
the Hold box and bring back whatever was in there. Every control is reachable
with one thumb in portrait, and nothing sits under the status bar or the
gesture area at the bottom of the screen.

The Rules
A piece falls one row on every tick of a gravity timer. It may not move into an
occupied cell or through the walls of the well; a rotation that would collide is
refused, then retried one column left and one column right before it is
abandoned. When a piece can fall no further it locks into the grid after a short
grace window, and the next piece enters. Any row that becomes completely full is
cleared: it flashes once, is removed, and every row above it drops down one.
Clearing one, two, three or four rows at once scores progressively more, and
four at once is worth far more than four singles. Every ten cleared lines the
level goes up by one and the gravity timer gets faster. If a newly entered piece
has nowhere legal to sit, the run is over.

The Session
A run starts at level one and ends when the well tops out. The goal is simply to
survive and to keep clearing lines: the game ends with a summary card showing the
final score, the lines cleared and the level reached, over a Play Again button
and a Quit button. A pause button in the status bar freezes the gravity timer and
dims the well behind a small pause panel with Resume and Quit.

Look and Feel
Dark, calm, high-contrast. A near-black background, a slightly lighter well, and
saturated flat block colours with a thin lighter edge on each cell so a stack
reads clearly at arm's length on a phone screen. Readout panels are flat dark
cards with pale numerals. Large type, generous spacing, no clutter and no
decoration that is not information. The whole layout scales to any phone aspect
ratio and keeps its margins clear of notches and rounded corners.

Audio
A quiet low drone under everything. A soft click when a piece shifts or rotates,
a firmer thud when it locks, a bright ascending chime when a row clears and a
louder four-note flourish when four clear at once. A short descending tone when
the well tops out.

Platform
This ships to Android phones only, targeting Android 12 and newer. Portrait
orientation, touch input only — no keyboard, no gamepad, no mouse. It must run
smoothly on a mid-range phone.

What The Game Is Made Of
This section exists because the first build described the screen but forgot the
game. Read it as the cast list.

The falling blocks are the game. They are not scenery and not a background
picture: they are the objects that exist, move, and are acted on, and there
should be many of them on screen at once. Every locked block stays in the well
as a real object until the row it sits in is cleared. The seven shapes — bar,
square, T, S, Z, L and J — are the kinds of piece that get spawned. A piece is
the thing the player touches, and touching it is how the whole game is played;
if nothing on screen can be touched and moved, there is no game.

The well itself, the Next box, the Hold box and the three readouts are the
frame around that. They display; they are not what the player acts on.

What The Player Is Trying To Do
The run has an explicit goal, and it is reached in stages rather than all at
once. Clear ten rows to finish the first stage and reach level two. Clear ten
more to reach level three, with the pieces falling faster each time. Survive
all of it without the stack reaching the top of the well. Each of these is a
thing the player is working on, one after the other, and the game should say
which one is current.

What The Core System Does
One system owns the rules of the well, and it does all of this, not just keep a
score: it holds a ten-by-twenty grid of cells and knows which are filled; it
spawns the next piece at the top; it steps the active piece down one row on
each tick of a gravity timer; it refuses a move or a rotation that would put a
cell into a wall, the floor or an occupied cell; it locks the piece into the
grid when it can fall no further; it finds every full row, removes it, and
drops everything above it down; it adds to the score by how many rows went at
once; it raises the level and shortens the gravity interval every ten rows; and
it ends the run when a newly spawned piece has nowhere legal to sit.

Nothing Else Belongs Here
There is no vehicle in this game, no terrain, no landscape and no world to
drive or walk through. Nothing in it has wheels. The only moving things are the
blocks in the well.
