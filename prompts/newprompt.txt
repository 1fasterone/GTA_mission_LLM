EMBERFALL KEEP is a 3D third-person action-adventure for PC. The player is the last knight of a mountain keep under siege by a fire cult that has broken the outer wall — fighting through burning courtyards and a collapsing tower, protecting the wounded garrison, and rushing to ring the chapel bell before the keep falls to ash. The tone is heavy on weight and impact: ringing steel, roaring fire, desperate shouting, a tolling bell counting down how much time is left.

Player mechanics: third-person walk locomotion with gravity throughout, sprint, jump, a raised-shield block, and light/heavy sword attacks. The camera is a Cinemachine third-person rig that tightens its field of view during combat lock-on. Input is handled through Unity's Input System package for responsive combat controls.

Key systems this keep needs: a health system tracking damage and death for the knight and the fire cultists; a siege countdown timer that escalates enemy waves the longer the player takes; a garrison morale counter that rises as wounded soldiers are rescued and gates the ending; NavMesh-driven cultist AI that patrols, goes alert, charges, and attacks, fleeing when set alight; voiced NPC dialogue for three garrison survivors — a hoarse sergeant quest-giver, a gruff quartermaster vendor, and an urgent field medic; an inventory system for sword, shield, torches, and healing draughts; particle VFX for parried sword sparks, cultist death fire bursts, falling debris when the tower cracks, and a damage flash; a Timeline-driven collapsing-tower sequence; and fade scene transitions across six keep areas (menu, courtyard breach, great hall, collapsing tower, inner sanctum, chapel bell).

Render pipeline: URP with bloom for firelight, volumetric smoke, and warm god-rays, on Unity 6000.0. Visual style is gritty medieval realism — firelight orange against cold stone blue, a night siege thick with smoke. Recommended packages: Cinemachine, Input System, NavMesh (AI Navigation), Timeline, Animation Rigging, and Addressables for the six-scene load.
