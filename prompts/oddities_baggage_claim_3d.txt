TITLE
Oddities Baggage Claim

GENRE
A fully 3D first-person exploration and sorting game set inside a flooded
airport baggage claim that has become a black-market lost-and-found for cursed
objects. The tone is strange, tactile, and cluttered rather than horror: more
midnight flea market than haunted house.

CORE PREMISE
The player is the night-shift claims clerk in a terminal where luggage from
impossible places keeps arriving on the carousel. Each round, the loudspeaker
announces three specific lost objects that must be found, scanned, stamped, and
loaded onto a rolling brass luggage cart before the terminal floods too high.
The player walks through the claim hall, scans suspicious items with a handheld
UV tag reader, carries one object at a time, and sorts each object onto the
correct colored inspection table.

The game should visibly fill the world with distinct physical clutter, not one
generic prop pile. The claim hall has soaked blue benches, dented luggage carts,
orange traffic cones, broken umbrella stands, rubber duck displays, brass
gramophones, cracked vending machines, folding chairs, dripping potted palms,
airport stanchions with velvet ropes, cardboard boxes, plastic security bins,
payphones, wall clocks stuck at different times, tangled extension cords,
janitor buckets, mop handles, wet floor signs, lost shoes, toy trains, porcelain
swans, cassette tapes, fishbowls, mannequin hands, glass jars of buttons,
passport kiosks, paper ticket rolls, coffee cups, newspapers, flashlight
batteries, skull-shaped snow globes, ceramic frogs, old cameras, violin cases,
rusted bird cages, red toolboxes, and stacks of striped suitcases.

The impossible baggage carousel is the centerpiece: it slowly carries glowing
suitcases, wooden crates, antique trunks, duffel bags, wrapped parcels, and a
few obviously wrong things like a bathtub with airline tags, a giant chess
knight, a miniature lighthouse, and a mossy stone doorway. A few harmless
lost-property inspectors wander the aisles with clipboards. If the player
scans an object that does not match the announcement, it rattles loudly and
slides back onto the carousel.

Do not try to include every object in the list above. Choose 18-24 distinct
physical object groups that best communicate the terminal, and make each group
a concrete thing such as "wet floor signs", "passport kiosks", or "ceramic
frogs". Do not use vague groups named Props, Clutter, Decorations, LostItems,
or SetDressing.

Gameplay needs a clear loop: listen to the loudspeaker, identify the right
objects by inspecting the clutter, scan them, carry them to the right table,
stamp the claim ticket, and load them onto the cart. The win condition is
sorting all announced objects before the water reaches the top of the benches.
The lose condition is letting the claim hall flood or stamping too many wrong
items. Use 3D models for the clutter wherever possible, including weird
Sketchfab-style objects that are unlikely to exist in the default model pack.
