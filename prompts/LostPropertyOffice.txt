TITLE: Platform Four Lost Property

CORE PREMISE
You are the clerk on the late shift at a city railway station's lost property
office. Nobody walks anywhere in this game and there is no character to steer.
You stand at the counter for the whole shift and work with the mouse and the
keyboard: the world of the game is the counter in front of you, the labelled
shelves behind it, and the claim ledger you write the day into before you go
home.

GENRE: a calm sorting and paperwork puzzle, played entirely through the
interface.

STYLE: warm and cluttered, hand-lettered card labels, brown paper tags, a
green desk lamp, the look of a real back office at eleven at night.

THE SHIFT
A porter tips the day's finds onto the counter in one heap and leaves. Behind
the counter are three shelves, each hand-labelled for one kind of thing. On the
end of the counter sits the claim ledger, which is a running list of everything
you have booked in tonight. The ledger is a record — it fills itself as you
write to it, and you cannot drop anything onto it by hand.

WHAT IS ON THE COUNTER
Lost umbrellas, still wet, several of them.
Lost suitcases, a few, one of them very heavy.
Lost phones, a small pile of them, all switched off.

WHAT IS BEHIND THE COUNTER
The umbrella shelf, a tall narrow rack.
The suitcase shelf, a deep low rack.
The phone shelf, a shallow tray of pigeonholes.

HOW THE SHIFT GOES
The shift has three parts and you work through them in order.

First, clear the counter. Every lost umbrella belongs on the umbrella shelf,
every lost suitcase on the suitcase shelf, every lost phone on the phone shelf.
Nothing goes on the wrong shelf — the labels are the whole point of the job.

Second, book the shelved things in. Once a kind of thing is all on its shelf,
you record it in the claim ledger by typing at the terminal. The ledger is
written, never dropped into.

Third, close the office. When every kind of thing has been shelved and booked,
you stamp the night's ledger and the shift is over.

RULES
There is nothing to fight, nothing to dodge and nothing to time. The only way
to fail is to put a thing where it does not belong, and the game tells you why
it refused rather than just snapping the thing back.

Every action is either a drag with the mouse or a word typed at the terminal,
and both do exactly the same thing to the office.
